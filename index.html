About

A little more detail

I am a machine learning engineer based in Kampala. My time goes to three places: Moja Remit, where I work on the integration layer of Moja Soko, a cross-border B2B payments and sourcing platform; Sunbird AI, where I design and run the production systems behind real-time translation and speech recognition for Ugandan languages; and Makerere University, where I am a graduate researcher and am joining the Makerere Safe Intelligence Lab.

The building is the part I actually enjoy. A model that only exists in a notebook helps nobody, so most of my work is the unglamorous half: getting something from a promising result to a service that stays up, logs what it is doing, and can be improved on evidence rather than on vibes. I work closely with linguists and domain experts throughout, because the hardest problems here are rarely only technical.

I also build my own things. I co-founded NexVox AI, where I am CTO, and founded Crested Haus — and under Crested Haus I am building Katale AI, a WhatsApp bot that takes payments and manages stock by text or voice note. None of it is incidental to the day job: it is where I find out what actually survives contact with users.

Shipping a model is the easy part. Being able to say honestly how well it works and where it fails is the hard part — and most of the interesting questions in AI safety start exactly there.

That thread runs through everything I do. My M.Sc. work is direct Luganda-to-English speech-to-speech translation and the safety questions that come with it: how to detect bias in a translation system, how to evaluate one honestly when it will be used in a medical or legal setting, and what alignment means across cultures rather than within one. In practice that means building evaluation harnesses — BLEU, chrF, COMET — so there is a real number to argue with instead of a demo. I am now starting research at the Makerere Safe Intelligence Lab on agentic AI, finance, and what AI safety means in African contexts specifically.

I write about this as well. Not polished retrospectives — more the notes I would have wanted when I started: what breaks when you point a model trained on English at a language with a fraction of the data, why evaluation is harder than it looks, and what AI safety means when you are the person deploying the thing rather than theorising about it. Writing is mostly how I work out what I think.

Before this I built enterprise CRM systems at Pahappa, applied computer vision to health datasets at Emergent AI, and worked on crop-disease detection at the Makerere AI Lab — which became my first two publications.

Selected work

Five things I built, and what came of them

Most of my work sits at the boundary between a model and the people using it. These are the projects where that boundary mattered most.

01 / Payments infrastructure

Moja Soko — cross-border B2B payment orchestration

Moja Remit · 2026 – present

NestJS TypeScript PostgreSQL Prisma Transactional outbox Auth0 Next.js
Problem
Cross-border B2B trade runs on invoices, FX and settlement spread across providers that agree on nothing — and the platform has to orchestrate all of it without ever holding the money itself.
What I did
Integration engineering on Moja Core, the NestJS and PostgreSQL backend behind the platform. External providers — payments, banking, marketplace sourcing, KYC and identity — sit behind typed adapter interfaces, so none of them leak into the domain. Financial state changes only through verified, signed, deduplicated webhooks and a transactional outbox, never from a browser request.
Outcome
A non-custodial sourcing, invoicing and payment-orchestration platform running on a deployed preview, with money held as integer minor units and FX rates as scaled integers — arithmetic in BigInt, rounded once, never a float.
mojasoko.com

02 / Agentic AI

Katale AI — an agent that handles money without touching it

Crested Haus · WhatsApp voice commerce agent

TypeScript Fastify LLM agents Whisper PayPal PostgreSQL Next.js
Problem
Small merchants here sell through conversations, not storefronts. Orders arrive as voice notes, totals are done by hand, and nobody knows the week's revenue without scrolling the chat. The obvious fix — put a language model on WhatsApp — is also the obvious way to lose money.
What I did
Built the agent so the model never moves money. Its tools are typed read, write and propose; the model interprets and drafts, backend services do the pricing and validation, and every consequential action waits on a one-time code bound to that exact payload. Voice notes go through Whisper; low-confidence speech is read back, and spoken approvals are refused outright.
Outcome
Live against PayPal Sandbox, with English text and voice commerce verified end to end — orders, payments, shipment tracking, dispute evidence and merchant analytics, all driven from a WhatsApp thread.
kataleai.space

03 / Production ML

Serving Sunbird's low-resource models

Sunbird AI · 2023 – present

Python PyTorch Model deployment Docker CI/CD Monitoring
Problem
Translation and speech recognition models for Ugandan languages were strong in evaluation but had no path to real users — no serving layer, no pipeline, no way to know when they degraded.
What I did
Designed and deployed the GPU-backed services and APIs serving real-time translation, ASR and text-to-speech, built end-to-end pipelines from collection through to deployment, and stood up monitoring for performance, reliability and usage.
Outcome
Models running in production with high availability, with BLEU, chrF and COMET evaluation so quality is benchmarked measurably rather than asserted.
Sunbird AI on GitHub

04 / Research

Speech-to-speech translation for Luganda

Makerere University · M.Sc. dissertation

Whisper LoRA / PEFT Transformers Speech recognition Machine translation
Problem
Speech translation assumes abundant paired audio and text. For Luganda that assumption does not hold, and the safety questions — bias, mistranslation in medical or legal settings — get harder, not easier, when data is scarce.
What I did
A direct Luganda-to-English speech-to-speech pipeline built on parameter-efficient adaptation of multimodal models, with the training datasets published openly, alongside work on bias detection and evaluation for high-stakes contexts.
Outcome
Dissertation submitted for examination, with the surrounding research published on African language coverage in LLMs and on speech data requirements for ASR.
